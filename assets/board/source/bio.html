A director who has governed at scale, rebuilt audit and risk oversight, and designed control systems from the ground up.

Fiduciary governance

Allison Alimayu was elected in 2012 to the Board of Regents of the Nevada System of Higher Education, the governing board of a multi-billion-dollar public system of eight colleges, universities, and research institutions. She served from 2013 to 2019, including as vice chair of the board and chair of the Audit and Compliance Committee, with fiduciary oversight of the chancellor and system executive leadership.

As audit chair, she led a redesign of the system's audit and enterprise risk management framework, including a staffing model that paired auditors with subject matter experts in each key area of enterprise risk. She directed a $25 million distribution from the Operating Pool Reserve to clear a backlog of internal audit follow-up and reduced open audit exceptions by 68 percent. On major capital and contract decisions, from the launch of a new medical school to a stadium joint use agreement, she required scenario modeling, firm cost targets, and exit protections before approval. She received the Distinguished Public Service Award in 2019 and has published on audit committee governance in the College and University Auditor Journal and Trustee Quarterly.

Financial and operating leadership

Over twelve years at UnitedHealthcare and DaVita Integrated Care (2006 to 2018), she rose through multiple internal promotions to design and lead a value-based reimbursement program across Arizona, Florida, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Texas that averaged $8.5 million in monthly savings.

System design and risk mitigation

As a 2025 to 2026 Fulbright US Scholar at Research Ireland, the national research funding agency, she designed SAFE, an end-to-end governance framework for challenge funding. She identified six design weaknesses that put outcomes at risk and built a control for each: a documented selection process with leadership sign-off, stage gates with an auditable decision record, independent review panels, a standing agreement on intellectual property, data, AI, and conflicts of interest, and a funded transition plan before any exit. She validated the framework against national statute and agency strategy.

Regulatory service

She is a voting member of the Medicare Evidence Development and Coverage Advisory Committee (MEDCAC), appointed by the US Secretary of Health and Human Services (2019 to 2027), and a commissioner on the North Carolina Oil and Gas Commission (2026 to 2030). She was an expert reviewer for the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change in 2026. Since 2018 she has led Color Lens Consulting, an independent healthcare and policy advisory practice.
