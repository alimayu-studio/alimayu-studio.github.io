FAAITH and HOPE

Every week, families bring their newborns to be blessed. FAAITH and HOPE turns that sacred moment into the start of a healthier childhood.

Why congregations

The most trusted room a family walks into

Many families, especially Black and brown families, face real barriers to care, from long waits to being dismissed by providers. Public health often plans by zip code. But families describe themselves by their faith, their church, and their people. Clergy are present for the grandmother, the grandchild, and the great-grandchild. FAAITH and HOPE starts there.

The CREED model

From blessing to a healthier childhood

Congregational Relationships for Early-childhood Equity and Development (CREED) weaves early childhood science into rituals that faith communities have practiced for centuries.

  1. PrepareBefore the blessing, families take part in short sessions on early brain development, safe sleep, and positive parenting.
  2. VisitTrained members of the congregation visit families at home, bringing trust that outside programs have to earn.
  3. BlessClergy perform the baby blessing in front of the whole faith community, which surrounds the family with support.
  4. AdvocateCongregations use what families share to push for fair access to care, services, and resources.

What your community receives

The CREED toolkit

Built with clergy and community members, tested at Shiloh Baptist Church, and informed by Dr. Bruce Perry of the Child Trauma Academy and Dr. Charlyn Harper Browne of the Center for the Study of Social Policy.

i.

Clergy training and guidebook

A curriculum that equips ordained clergy and faith leaders to teach early brain development, positive parenting, and the HOPE framework as part of the ministry they already lead.

ii.

Parent leadership and advocacy series

A five-part, faith-centered series with a family workbook and slides. Parents learn to build positive childhood experiences, strengthen resilience, and speak up for their children.

iii.

REAL Impact survey

The Racial Equity Assessment and Learning survey shows how systemic racism shows up when families seek care, including being dismissed or not taken seriously, so congregations can act on real data.

iv.

Systems Change Guidebook

Step-by-step guidance for congregations to work with health systems and policymakers on the barriers their families name.

The story

A pastor, a researcher, and a question

Rev. Dr. Darrell LaRue Armstrong has pastored Shiloh Baptist Church, Trenton’s first Black Baptist church, since 2000. A former assistant commissioner over New Jersey’s Division of Child Abuse Prevention and board president of the Association of Professionals Solving the Abuse of Children (APSAC), he saw each baby blessing as a missed chance to share what science knows about a child’s first years.

Dr. Alimayu, then directing networks and policy at the HOPE National Resource Center at Tufts Medical Center, partnered with him to formalize that work into a model any faith tradition could use. With funding from the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation, they tested it together as co-investigators.
