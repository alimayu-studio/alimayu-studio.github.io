Data Centers and Community Health: What Local Boards and Residents Should Ask Before Approving a Site

Data centers bring tax revenue, and they also bring generators, noise, water use, and grid demand. These questions belong in the record before a vote.

By Dr. Allison Alimayu · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

Data centers are arriving in counties across the country, often on fast timelines and under nondisclosure agreements. Local boards are asked to decide quickly. Health questions often come last, if at all.

Questions About Air

  • How many diesel backup generators will the site have, and how many hours a year are they permitted to run?
  • Who monitors emissions, and will the results be public?
  • Which homes, schools, and child care centers sit downwind?

Questions About Noise and Light

  • What sound levels will reach the nearest homes, day and night, including low-frequency hum?
  • What lighting will run all night, and how will it be shielded?

Questions About Water and Power

  • How much water will cooling use on the hottest days, and from what source?
  • Who pays for new power lines and plants, and could residential rates rise?

Questions About Accountability

  • Has a health impact assessment been done?
  • Is there a community benefit agreement, and who enforces it?
  • What happens if the operator does not meet its commitments?
