The Standard of Care: How to Ask for the Care Medical Guidelines Recommend

Medicine has written guidelines for most common conditions. Patients have every right to ask whether their care follows them, and to act when it does not.

By Allison Alimayu, PhD · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

For most common conditions, expert groups publish guidelines that spell out what good care looks like. These guidelines set the standard of care. Every patient deserves care that meets it.

Where the Guidelines Come From

  • US Preventive Services Task Force: screening recommendations for conditions such as high blood pressure, diabetes, and depression.
  • Medical specialty groups: for example, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists publishes guidance on care after birth.
  • The CDC: vaccine schedules for children and adults.

Many guidelines are free to read online. A search for the condition plus the word "guidelines" is a good start.

Questions to Ask at a Visit

  • What do the guidelines recommend for someone with these symptoms or this history?
  • Does this plan of care follow those guidelines?
  • If not, what is the reason, and will it be written in the chart?
  • Which tests or follow-up visits should happen next, and when?

A good doctor can answer these questions plainly. A short visit is no excuse for skipping a recommended screening, test, or referral.

When Care Falls Short

  • Write down what happened, with dates, while it is fresh.
  • Request a copy of the medical record. Under HIPAA, providers generally must respond within 30 days.
  • Ask for a different doctor, or get a second opinion.
  • File a complaint with the practice, the hospital's patient advocate, or the health plan.
  • For serious concerns, file a complaint with the state medical board, which licenses doctors.

Asking for the standard of care is not being difficult. It is how patients and families make sure care is safe, fair, and complete.

For education only. This article does not diagnose or treat any condition and is not medical advice. Talk with a clinician about symptoms and test results. In an emergency, call 911.
