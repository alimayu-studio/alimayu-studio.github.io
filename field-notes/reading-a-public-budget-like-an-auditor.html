Reading a Public Budget Like an Auditor: Questions Every Candidate and Board Member Should Ask

A public budget tells the story of what a community values. These questions help candidates and board members read it with care.

By Dr. Allison Alimayu · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

A budget is a policy document written in numbers. Reading it well is one of the most useful skills a candidate or board member can build.

Start With the Big Picture

  • What are the largest sources of money, and how stable are they?
  • What are the largest costs, and which ones grow every year on their own?
  • Is the budget balanced with one-time money, like reserves or grants?

Follow the Changes

  • Compare this year to last year and to what was actually spent.
  • Ask about any line that moved more than 10 percent.
  • Look for programs that shrank without a public decision.

Ask Like an Auditor

  • What did the last audit find, and were the findings fixed?
  • Who can approve spending, and what controls are in place?
  • What risks are not in the budget yet, like lawsuits, repairs, or expiring grants?

Bring It Back to People

Every line serves someone. The best budget questions connect the numbers to children and families, workers, and neighborhoods.
