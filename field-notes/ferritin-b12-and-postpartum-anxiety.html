Low Ferritin, Low B12, or Postpartum Anxiety? Ruling Out Physical Causes of Mood Changes

No lab test can diagnose postpartum depression or anxiety. Some physical problems can cause or worsen mood changes, though, and they are worth ruling out.

By Allison Alimayu, PhD · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

No blood test can diagnose postpartum depression or anxiety. That point matters. Mood changes after birth are real, common, and treatable, and they deserve care whether or not a lab result is off.

At the same time, some physical problems can make mood feel worse, and they are easy to check.

Physical Causes Worth Ruling Out

  • Low iron stores (ferritin): can bring tiredness, poor focus, a racing heart, and restless sleep.
  • Low vitamin B12 or folate: can bring tiredness, brain fog, and low mood.
  • Thyroid changes: too much hormone can feel like anxiety, and too little can feel like depression.
  • Low vitamin D: common after pregnancy and in winter months.

A Screening Questionnaire Helps Too

Clinicians use a 10-question survey called the Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale. Mental Health America offers it free and in private online. Sharing the score with a clinician is a good next step.

When to Get Help Right Away

  • National Maternal Mental Health Hotline: call or text 1-833-852-6262, any time.
  • Postpartum Support International Helpline: call or text 1-800-944-4773.
  • Thoughts of self-harm or harming the baby: call or text 988, or call 911.

For education only. This article does not diagnose or treat any condition and is not medical advice. Talk with a clinician about symptoms and test results. In an emergency, call 911.
