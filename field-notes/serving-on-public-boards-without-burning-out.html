Serving on Public Boards Without Burning Out: Practical Governance for Community Leaders

Public board service asks for steady judgment over years. A few habits protect both the board's work and the person doing it.

By Dr. Allison Alimayu · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

A seat on a public board can be one of the most meaningful roles in civic life. It can also wear people down: long packets, late meetings, and public pressure, often unpaid.

Know the Job

  • Boards set direction and hold leaders accountable. Staff run the day to day.
  • Read the bylaws, the open meetings law, and the ethics rules in the first month.
  • Know which decisions belong to the board and which do not.

Protect Time

  • Block time to read the board packet before each meeting.
  • Pick one or two committees and go deep, instead of spreading thin.
  • Ask staff for a short summary on long items.

Ask Better Questions

  • What problem does this solve, and how will we know it worked?
  • What does it cost over five years, not just this year?
  • Who is affected, and did they have a chance to weigh in?

Stay Grounded

Disagreement is part of the work. Keep critique focused on the issue, keep notes, and keep a few people outside the board who can offer honest counsel.
