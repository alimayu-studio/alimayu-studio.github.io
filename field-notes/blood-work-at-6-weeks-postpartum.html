What Blood Work to Ask For at 6 Weeks Postpartum (And What Routine Checkups Miss)

The six-week visit often ends without a single lab test. Here are the blood tests worth asking about, and why they matter in the first months after birth.

By Allison Alimayu, PhD · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

For many mothers, the six-week visit is the last scheduled stop in postpartum care. It often covers healing, birth control, and mood, and then it ends. Blood work is not always part of it, even though several common problems after birth show up first in the blood.

Why Labs Matter This Early

Birth can bring real blood loss. Pregnancy and breastfeeding draw down iron, vitamin D, and B12. The thyroid can shift in the first months after birth. None of these always cause clear symptoms, and many get brushed off as normal new-parent tiredness.

Tests Worth Asking About

  • Complete blood count (CBC): checks for anemia after blood loss.
  • Ferritin: shows stored iron. It can run low before a CBC looks abnormal.
  • TSH and free T4: a first look at thyroid changes after birth.
  • Metabolic panel: kidney, liver, blood sugar, and salts, which matters after preeclampsia or a hard delivery.
  • Vitamin D, B12, and folate: nutrients that breastfeeding and pregnancy use up.

If She Had Gestational Diabetes

National guidelines call for a glucose test between 4 and 12 weeks after birth for anyone who had gestational diabetes. The preferred test is a two-hour glucose tolerance test. It is easy to miss, so it is worth asking about by name.

How to Ask

  • Bring a short written list of the tests to the visit.
  • Name any symptoms: tiredness, dizziness, hair loss, a racing heart, or feeling low.
  • Ask for copies of the results, not only "they looked fine."

For education only. This article does not diagnose or treat any condition and is not medical advice. Talk with a clinician about symptoms and test results. In an emergency, call 911.
