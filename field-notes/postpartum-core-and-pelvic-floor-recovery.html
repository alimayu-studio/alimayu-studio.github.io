Beyond the 6-Week Clearance: Practical Steps for Postpartum Core and Pelvic Floor Recovery

Being cleared at six weeks does not mean the body is done healing. Here is what pelvic floor and core recovery can look like, and the signs that call for an expert.

By Allison Alimayu, PhD · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

"Cleared for activity" at six weeks is a starting line, not a finish line. The core and pelvic floor carry a pregnancy for months, and they often need months to rebuild.

Signs That Call for an Expert

  • Leaking urine with a cough, sneeze, laugh, or jump
  • A feeling of heaviness or bulging in the pelvis
  • Pain with sex or with using tampons
  • A gap or doming along the middle of the belly when sitting up
  • Low back or hip pain that does not ease

These are common, but they are not something to simply live with. A pelvic floor physical therapist can assess and treat them. In many places, no referral is needed.

Gentle Starting Points

  • Breathing that lets the belly and pelvic floor rise and fall together
  • Walking, building slowly in time and pace
  • Avoiding heavy straining and breath-holding while lifting
  • Adding strength work in stages, with expert guidance when symptoms show up

Give It Time

Recovery after birth takes about a year, and it does not follow a straight line. Steady, small steps usually beat a fast return to hard workouts.

For education only. This article does not diagnose or treat any condition and is not medical advice. Talk with a clinician about symptoms and test results. In an emergency, call 911.
