How Health Care Payment Models Shape What Families Actually Receive at the Clinic

How a clinic gets paid shapes how long a visit lasts, which tests are offered, and who gets a call back. Here is how the main models work.

By Dr. Allison Alimayu · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

Families experience health care as visits, waits, and bills. Behind each visit is a payment model that shapes what happens in the room.

Fee-for-Service

The clinic is paid for each visit, test, or procedure. This can reward doing more, and it can leave little room for unpaid work like phone calls or care coordination.

Capitation

The clinic is paid a set amount per person per month. This can reward keeping people healthy. It can also reward doing less if quality is not tracked.

Value-Based Care

Payment is tied to results, like fewer hospital stays or better screening rates. This can improve care, but it depends on measuring the right things fairly.

What It Means for Families

  • Short visits often reflect payment pressure, not a lack of care.
  • Services like care coordinators and home visits often depend on how a clinic is paid.
  • Asking what a plan covers, and what support comes with it, can open doors.
