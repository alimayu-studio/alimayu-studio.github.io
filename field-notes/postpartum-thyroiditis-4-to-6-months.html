Postpartum Thyroiditis: Why Sudden Exhaustion and Hair Loss Often Hit at 4 to 6 Months

Thyroid changes after birth are common and easy to miss. Here is how the two phases work and when a simple blood test can help.

By Allison Alimayu, PhD · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

Many mothers feel worse at four or five months than they did at six weeks. Sleep loss gets the blame. Sometimes the cause is the thyroid, a small gland in the neck that sets the body's pace.

Two Phases

Postpartum thyroiditis is swelling of the thyroid in the year after birth. It often moves in two phases:

  • Too much thyroid hormone (often months 1 to 4): a racing heart, feeling shaky or wired, trouble sleeping, and weight loss.
  • Too little thyroid hormone (often months 4 to 8): heavy tiredness, feeling cold, weight gain, brain fog, and low mood.

Some women go through only one phase. Most recover within the year, but some stay low and need ongoing care.

What About Hair Loss?

Shedding around three to four months after birth is common and usually normal, as hair that held on during pregnancy lets go. Shedding that is heavy, lasts a long time, or comes with other symptoms is worth a thyroid check.

Who Is More Likely to Have It

  • Women with thyroid antibodies (TPO antibodies)
  • Women with type 1 diabetes or another autoimmune condition
  • Women who had thyroiditis after an earlier birth

The Test

A TSH and free T4 blood test shows how the thyroid is working. A TPO antibody test shows who is at higher risk. Low mood in the hypothyroid phase can look like postpartum depression, so ruling out the thyroid is a good step.

For education only. This article does not diagnose or treat any condition and is not medical advice. Talk with a clinician about symptoms and test results. In an emergency, call 911.
