Getting Ballot-Ready: What First-Time Candidates Need to Know Before Filing Day

Most first campaigns stumble on paperwork and timing, not ideas. Here is what to settle before filing day.

By Dr. Allison Alimayu · November 17, 2026 · 2 minute read

Filing day is the moment a campaign becomes real. The work that decides whether it goes well happens months before.

Check the Basics First

  • Eligibility: age, residency, and registration rules for the seat.
  • The district: confirm the exact lines, since they change after redistricting.
  • The calendar: filing opens and closes on set dates, and late filings are rejected.
  • The cost of filing: a fee, a petition, or both, depending on the office and the state.

Set Up the Money Side Early

  • Learn when a campaign committee must register and who can serve as treasurer.
  • Open a separate campaign bank account.
  • Track every dollar from the start, because reporting deadlines come fast.

Build the Base Before Announcing

  • A short list of people who will help in the first two weeks
  • A one-sentence reason for running
  • A plan for time, family, and work during the campaign

Ask the Election Office

The local board of elections is the best source for rules and dates. Staff answer these questions every cycle.
