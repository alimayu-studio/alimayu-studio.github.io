A plain-language brief

Extreme heat and kidney health in Nevada

By Allison Alimayu, PhD

Heat is hard on kidneys

Kidneys clean the blood. When the body gets too hot and loses water, the kidneys work harder. For people with kidney disease, diabetes, or high blood pressure, a hot day can end in the emergency room.

What the study found

Dr. Alimayu studied emergency room visits in Nevada from 2016 to 2019. She compared them with a heat stress measure that counts heat, humidity, wind, and sunlight.

On hotter days, more people went to the emergency room for kidney problems. The link was strong.

More heat. More kidney emergencies.

Who faces the most risk

The Las Vegas and Reno areas faced the highest risk. Many families there have fewer resources and fewer health services close by.

Nevada had not used the CDC’s main heat and health programs. No kidney care guidelines told doctors to plan for heat.

Why this study matters

Before this study, no one had fully mapped heat and health risk across Nevada. Dr. Alimayu brought together weather data, emergency room visits, and information about each community, then mapped where people face the most risk.

The work was her doctoral research in biomedical informatics at Rutgers University.

A real example

In Florida, researchers studied farmworkers who spend long days in the heat. They found signs of kidney injury and weaker kidney function. Many Nevadans work outside or near hot machines, too.

What needs to happen

  • A state plan to protect people from heat
  • Training for doctors and nurses on heat and kidneys
  • A heat and health course for every health science student at Nevada’s public universities
  • Health plan coverage for kidney care during heat waves
